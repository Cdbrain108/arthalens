# Ratios in Plain Words (for you, and for the report's Beginner mode)

Use this as the source for the "Beginner Reading Guide" text. Every ratio: what it is, simple example, what is good, what to be careful about.

| Term | Plain meaning | Formula | Rough reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EPS** (earnings per share) | Profit that belongs to one share | Net profit / number of shares | Higher and rising is good |
| **P/E** (price to earnings) | How many rupees you pay for Rs 1 of yearly profit | Price / EPS | Low can mean cheap OR the market expects trouble. Compare with the same company's past and its peers, never in isolation |
| **P/B** (price to book) | Price compared with what the company's books say it owns (after debts) | Price / book value per share | High for asset-light companies like IT is normal |
| **ROE** (return on equity) | Profit made for every Rs 100 shareholders put in | Net profit / shareholder equity | Above ~15-20% is strong |
| **ROCE** | Same idea, but on all capital used (own + borrowed) | EBIT / capital employed | Higher is better |
| **Operating margin (OPM)** | Of every Rs 100 of sales, how much is left after running costs | Operating profit / revenue | Compare with similar companies |
| **Net profit margin** | Of every Rs 100 of sales, how much is final profit | Net profit / revenue | Same |
| **Debt / equity** | How much the company borrowed vs its own money | Debt / equity | Near 0 = little debt, low risk |
| **Dividend yield** | Cash payout per year as a % of the price | Yearly dividend / price | 2-5% is income-friendly |
| **Market cap** | Total price of all shares | Price x shares | Size of the company |
| **52-week range** | Lowest and highest price in the last year | - | Shows if price is near a bottom or top |
| **TCV** | Total value of deals signed (contracts) | - | Signed is not the same as earned revenue yet |
| **YoY / QoQ** | Change vs same period last year / vs last quarter | (New/Old - 1) x 100 | YoY removes seasonality |
| **Crore (Cr)** | 10 million (1 Cr = 10,000,000) | - | INR 72,275 Cr = INR 722,750 million |

## How to explain TCS with these (example sentences you can reuse)
- "For every Rs 100 of sales TCS keeps about Rs 24 as operating profit. Similar IT firms keep less, so this is a strength."
- "You pay about Rs 15 for every Rs 1 of TCS's yearly profit (P/E 15.1). That is lower than TCS has traded at in the past."
- "TCS has very little debt (debt/equity 0.10), so a bad year is unlikely to put it in financial trouble."
- "The share price is down about 25% in a year, mostly because of worries about AI cutting IT spending and the Tata Sons dispute, even though profits are still strong."

## Rule for the report
Every ratio shown in Expert mode must have: value, formula, inputs used, source, date. Beginner mode shows only the one-line meaning plus a Good / Okay / Watch tag.
